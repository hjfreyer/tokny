## Summary

FEMA's pre-disaster hazard mitigation grant for infrastructure projects
that reduce risk from natural hazards (flood protection, drainage,
culvert and bridge upgrades, retrofits, slope stabilization), with a
heavy emphasis on infrastructure/construction and building-code
adoption. Local governments apply as *subapplicants* through their
state; in New York that is DHSES.

Status history matters here. FEMA announced in April 2025 that it was
ending BRIC. A federal court found the termination unlawful (final
ruling December 11, 2025, permanent injunction) and in March 2026
ordered FEMA to issue the FY2024 NOFO. FEMA then issued a single
FY2024 & FY2025 NOFO ($1 billion; posted March 25, 2026; FEMA deadline
July 23, 2026). The CRS Insight of June 30, 2026
(https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2026-06-30_IN12609_09fb213d55c75ff020619e3f9cdbe2f7657bca05.html)
says no replacement program has been named, no separate FY2026 NOFO is
mentioned, and roughly $5.075B remains set aside for BRIC in the
Disaster Relief Fund. So: the program survived, one combined round ran
in spring–summer 2026, and whether/when a FY2026+ round opens is
unknown.

## Eligibility

grants.gov listing (read 2026-11-04): eligible applicants include
"City or township governments," with the note "Local governments must
apply through their state or territory." The North Carolina state
notice for the same NOFO
(https://www.ncdps.gov/our-organization/emergency-management/hazard-mitigation/non-disaster-grants/notice-funding-availability-bric-fy24-25)
summarizes the NOFO's subapplicant rules: local governments (cities,
counties, townships, special districts) are eligible; a FEMA-approved
Local Hazard Mitigation Plan under 44 CFR Part 201 is required by the
application deadline and at obligation; cost share is 75/25 (up to 90%
federal for small impoverished communities with documentation);
project cap $20M federal; management costs up to 5%.

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston is an eligible subapplicant through
DHSES, provided it is covered by a current FEMA-approved hazard
mitigation plan (normally the Ulster County multi-jurisdictional plan
— whether the town has adopted the current plan needs confirmation).

## Fit

A good vehicle for a flood-prone culvert, road, or drainage project if
one exists (needs confirmation). The "small impoverished community"
90% share could apply to a town of 900 if income criteria are met —
needs confirmation. Competitive at the national level; the $2M state
allocation is the realistic pot.

## Effort

Subapplication in FEMA GO (go.fema.gov) to DHSES ahead of the state's
own internal deadline (states ran LOI processes in spring 2026).
Requires UEI/SAM.gov, a login.gov account and an Authorized
Organizational Representative in FEMA GO (DHSES says registration can
take up to four weeks), a Benefit-Cost Analysis for construction
projects, environmental/historic review documentation, and the hazard
mitigation plan. DHSES's hazard mitigation page
(https://www.dhses.ny.gov/hazard-mitigation) said on 2026-11-04 "There
are currently no open funding opportunities" and that it expects to
announce programs in August/September.
