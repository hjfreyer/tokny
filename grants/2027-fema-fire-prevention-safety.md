## Summary

Companion to AFG for prevention rather than response: funding
categories are Community Risk Reduction, Wildfire Risk Reduction, Code
Enforcement/Awareness, Origin and Cause Investigation, and
National/State/Regional Programs, plus firefighter safety research.
FY2025 round: opened May 19, 2026, closed June 22, 2026, $32.4M,
maximum award $1.5M. The September 10, 2026 Federal Register notice
(https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-18419.pdf) sets
the FP&S cost share at a minimum of 5% of the grant, cash or in-kind
allowed, and the same 80% maintenance-of-effort rule as AFG. Annual
program; next round expected spring 2027.

## Eligibility

Per the Maine Office of Community Affairs summary of the FY2025 NOFO
(read 2026-11-04; the FEMA NOFO itself was not reachable): fire
departments; federally recognized tribes; and "national, regional,
state, local, tribal, and nonprofit organizations recognized for fire
prevention and safety expertise." A fire department "must have a
formal arrangement with a government authority to provide fire
suppression within a fixed first-due response area."

**Verdict:** Eligible if the Town of Kingston operates a fire
department or can show recognized fire-prevention expertise as a local
government — the former needs confirmation; the latter is unlikely for
a town with no code-enforcement fire marshal (needs confirmation).

## Fit

A community-risk-reduction project (smoke alarm installation, rural
address signage, chimney/heating safety outreach) is the plausible
use. Marginal unless the town is the fire-service provider.

## Effort

FEMA GO application in the AFG window; 5% match; narrative tied to a
community risk assessment. Help desk: FireGrants@fema.dhs.gov,
(866) 274-0960.
