## Summary

The Digital Equity Act's competitive grant for projects that help
covered populations (rural residents, older adults, veterans,
low-income households, etc.) get online and use the internet: device
access, digital skills training, public access points, broadband
adoption outreach. NTIA terminated the program on May 9, 2025; the
National Digital Inclusion Alliance sued; on July 15, 2026 a federal
judge held one statutory provision unconstitutional but severable. In
an August 3, 2026 status report Commerce said it "presently targets a
December 2026 opening date for the Competitive Grant Program
application." On August 25, 2026 NTIA published a Paperwork Reduction
Act notice on the revived program's application forms (comments due
October 26, 2026), per Blandin on Broadband
(https://blandinonbroadband.org/2026/08/25/ntia-takes-steps-to-reopen-the-digital-equity-competitive-grants/).
No NOFO was posted as of 2026-11-04.

## Eligibility

Per the Blandin summary of the NTIA notice (read 2026-11-04): eligible
applicant types are "State political subdivisions and
instrumentalities; Indian Tribes; Alaska Native entities; Native
Hawaiian organizations; nonprofits that are not schools; community
anchor institutions; local educational agencies; workforce development
entities; and partnerships among them." Benton's August 4, 2026 post
confirms local governments are covered by the statute and notes the
2024 round "mostly funds cities and nonprofit entities."

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston, as a political subdivision of the
state, is an eligible applicant type. The practical fit for a town
this size is as a partner in a library-, county-, or nonprofit-led
application rather than as lead.

## Fit

A town-hall public Wi-Fi/computer access point or a senior digital
skills program would qualify in kind; whether there is local demand
and a partner (library, county) needs confirmation. Prior awards went
mostly to larger entities.

## Effort

Unknown until the NOFO is published (target December 2026). Expect
SAM.gov/UEI, alignment with New York's State Digital Equity Plan, and
outcome metrics for covered populations.
