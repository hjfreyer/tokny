## Summary

DEC's Climate Smart Communities (CSC) grant funds municipal
implementation projects that cut greenhouse gas emissions (mainly outside
the power sector) or adapt to climate change, plus planning/inventory/
assessment projects aligned with CSC certification actions. The 2026
notice lists example topics: reducing vehicle miles traveled, reducing
food waste to landfill, cutting HFC emissions from refrigeration/cooling,
restoring natural features for resiliency, reducing flood risk
(including relocating or retrofitting critical infrastructure),
preparing for extreme heat/weather, renewable energy at municipal
facilities, and engineering feasibility studies for flood risk, refrigerant
management and smart growth. 50/50 matching grant. Applications go only
through the CFA.

The 2026 round (DEC's page calls it Round 10) closed July 31, 2026. The
program has run annually through the CFA; next window expected with the
2027 CFA (historically opens ~May/June, closes end of July).

## Eligibility

Program page (read 2026-11-04): "Cities, towns, villages, counties,
Indian Nations, local public authorities, and local public benefit
corporations of the State of New York" are eligible. School districts,
BOCES, fire districts and libraries are not eligible on their own but
"may apply in partnership with an eligible lead municipality".
Municipalities "need not be registered or certified as a Climate Smart
Community to apply for a grant." Applicants "must be registered in the
New York Statewide Financial System Grants Management System to be
eligible."

ENB notice 2026-06-03
(https://dec.ny.gov/news/environmental-notice-bulletin/2026-06-03/public-notice/statewide-round-9-of-the-climate-smart-communities-grant-program-to-open-july-31-2026):
"County, city, town, village, local public authorities, local public
benefit corporation of the State of New York or Indian Nation within the
boundaries of New York State are eligible to apply." At least $17M for
implementation; implementation awards $35,000–$2,000,000; planning
awards $10,000–$200,000 (up to $1M total).

**Verdict: Town of Kingston is eligible** (towns are named explicitly;
CSC registration is not required).

## Fit

Flood-risk reduction, culvert/critical-infrastructure retrofit, and
municipal-building renewable energy are all in scope and are the kind of
project a small town could float at the $35k floor. The 50% match is the
main obstacle; whether the town has a project and match needs
confirmation. Whether the town is a registered CSC (not required, but
planning grants must align with CSC actions) needs confirmation.

## Effort

- Apply through the CFA (apps.cio.ny.gov/apps/cfa/); no mail/email.
- Must be registered in the SFS Grants Management System.
- Towns "must submit a resolution authorizing the application and
  specifying the local match amount and source" (program page).
- Work plan, expenditure-based budget, and certification forms (sexual
  harassment prevention, Executive Order 16, gender-based violence in the
  workplace).
- Applicant must own the project property or obtain a climate change
  mitigation easement.
- Program page notes changes to 6 NYCRR Part 492 effective April 1, 2026
  affect the CSC grant; read the current RFA.
