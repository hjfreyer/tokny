## Summary

EPF-funded competitive grant for dam removal and **stream crossing
(culvert) replacement and design** on Hudson estuary tributaries. Project
types in the RFA include: stream crossing replacement (construction to
replace/right-size one crossing); stream crossing design (one or several
crossings in the same municipality, leading to a shovel-ready project);
and combined design & replacement of a single crossing. Priority to
projects benefiting river herring and American eel and closest to tidal
waters. Awards $10,500–$200,000; up to two bids per applicant. Contract
term Sept 1, 2027 – Aug 31, 2030. Deadline 3:00 pm ET, December 17,
2026, via SFS (application ID HRE50 2026).

## Eligibility

RFA (read 2026-11-04): eligible bidders are "501(c)(3) not-for-profit
corporations, Governmental Entities, Municipalities, and
Quasi-Governmental Entities", which "also covers counties, cities, towns,
villages, recognized tribal nations, public authorities, soil and water
conservation districts, school districts, and universities and colleges,
provided the project is within the eligible boundaries." Projects must be
within the Hudson River Estuary watershed boundary (Appendix I /
interactive map). Match at least 10% of the requested budget; match
cannot come from other NYS or federal sources.

**Verdict: Town of Kingston is eligible** (towns named), subject to the
crossing being on a perennial stream inside the estuary watershed.

## Fit

A town highway department with an undersized or failing road culvert on
a perennial stream is exactly the target; the design-only type makes a
small first project possible. Whether the town has candidate crossings,
whether any have NAACC assessments, and whether 10% match is available
all need confirmation.

## Effort

- Register in SFS Grants Management; submit bid form via SFS by Dec 17,
  2026 (no paper/late bids). Questions accepted until Dec 10, 2026.
- Required: NAACC assessment results showing the crossing rated moderate/
  significant/severe (or priority in a plan/study, or water-quality/flood
  benefit); proof of ownership or landowner permission; FEMA floodplain
  map if applicable.
- Design standards: fully fish-passable; pass at least the 1% annual
  chance (100-year) storm; meet NYSDOT Highway Design Manual Ch. 8 and
  Protection of Waters permit minimums; assess upstream/downstream
  hydraulics.
- "Consult with Estuary Program staff as the first step."
- Certifications: sexual harassment prevention, EO 16, Gender-Based
  Violence (Attachment G), Smart Growth Assessment form.
- Completion by Aug 31, 2030.
