## Summary

USDA Rural Development's Community Facilities (CF) program funds the
purchase, construction, or improvement of "essential community
facilities" in rural areas, plus equipment and related project costs
(legal, engineering, architectural, environmental). Examples the program
page gives include public facilities, public safety services (fire,
rescue, EMS), community support services, health care, and educational
services. Grant assistance is a percentage of eligible cost, with the
rest financed as a direct loan (fixed rate, up to 40 years) or from
other sources. Applications are accepted year-round (October 1 –
September 30) through the local RD office. Priority goes to public
health and safety, energy efficiency, and education projects.

## Eligibility

From the program page (read 2026-11-04): eligible borrowers are "Public
bodies," "Community-based non-profit corporations," and "Federally
Recognized Tribes." Eligible areas are "rural areas including cities,
villages, townships and towns including Federally Recognized Tribal
Lands with no more than 20,000 residents according to the latest U.S.
Census Data." Applicants must be unable to finance the project from
their own resources or commercial credit at reasonable rates.

Grant tiers (both population and income tests must be met):

| Max grant | Population | Service-area median household income |
|---|---|---|
| 75% | ≤ 5,000 | below the higher of poverty line or 60% of state nonmetro median |
| 55% | ≤ 12,000 | ... or 70% |
| 35% | ≤ 20,000 | ... or 80% |
| 15% | ≤ 20,000 | ... or 90% |

The NY fact sheet (https://www.rd.usda.gov/files/NY_CF_Direct_Fact_Sheet.pdf)
confirms public bodies are eligible, that applications are accepted
year-round, and that NY applications are processed through the USDA RD
area office (state office: 441 S. Salina St., Syracuse, NY 13202,
315-477-6434).

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston is a public body in a town of ~900
residents, well under the 20,000 cap, so it qualifies as an applicant.
Which grant tier (if any) applies depends on the service-area median
household income, which needs confirmation.

## Fit

or building, or records facility; whether the town has such a project,
and whether its income profile earns a meaningful grant share rather
than mostly loan, needs confirmation.

## Effort

Per the program page: contact the local RD office first; obtain a
Unique Entity ID and SAM.gov registration; preparation "can take a few
weeks to several months." Public-body checklist is RD Form 1942-40.
The NY fact sheet describes a two-step pre-application then full
application, and construction projects must include a preliminary
architectural report. Applicant must document inability to get
commercial credit. (Town-side, not from the notice: a loan component
would need a board resolution and bonding authority.)
