## Summary

Bond Act-funded grants for large stormwater projects in flood-prone
communities that combine green and gray infrastructure to reduce flood
risk, improve water quality and build resilience (regional green
infrastructure such as floodplain/riparian/wetland restoration, or
local green infrastructure such as bioretention, porous pavement,
street trees). Opened Oct 7, 2026; due **4:00 pm, Friday, January 29,
2027**, via the EFC Customer Portal (portal.efc.ny.gov). At least 35% of
benefit must go to Disadvantaged Communities.

## Eligibility

ENB notice 2026-10-07
(https://dec.ny.gov/news/environmental-notice-bulletin/2026-10-07/public-notice/statewide-green-resiliency-grant-grg-program-45-million-available):
"Municipalities and State Entities as defined by NYS Environmental
Conservation Law Article 58." The April 2026 program summary says "All
applicants must be a municipality as defined by NYS Environmental
Conservation Law Article 58", which "includes local public authorities,
local governments, school districts, Indian nations or tribes, and State
agencies". Applicant must own the project site with rights to access,
operate and maintain it.

**Verdict: Town of Kingston is eligible** as a local government, but
note the $1,000,000 minimum award.

## Fit

Probably too large: the $1M floor and 10% match ($100k+) exceed what a
~900-resident town usually undertakes, and projects must reduce ≥2,500
cubic feet of Water Quality Volume and store a 10-year, 6-hour storm.
Only worth it if the town has a known flooding problem area and a
partner (county, SWCD). Needs confirmation.

## Effort

- Feasibility study following EFC's Feasibility Study Outline required
  with the application.
- Certification form signed by the highest elected official.
- Post-award: SEQRA/SHPO documentation, detailed budget and plan of
  finance, proof of access rights; construction must start within two
  years of award.
- Webinars: Oct 21, 2026 and Nov 4, 2026 (2–3 pm), registration on
  efc.ny.gov.
