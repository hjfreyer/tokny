## Summary

Grants to public bodies, nonprofits and tribes for projects that
benefit small and emerging rural businesses. Two categories. *Enterprise
grants* fund technical assistance and training, land and building
projects, equipment and utilities, pollution control, revolving loan
funds, rural distance learning, transportation improvements, community
and technology-based economic development, feasibility studies,
leadership training, business incubators, and long-term strategic
planning. *Opportunity grants* (capped at 10% of annual funding) fund
community/technology-based economic development, feasibility studies
and business plans, leadership training, incubators, and strategic
planning. There is no cost-share requirement, though committed
non-federal funds are an evaluation factor. The FY2026 window closed
June 30, 2026 (SECD applications June 15); it is an annual program.

## Eligibility

Program page (read 2026-11-04): "Grants may be made to a Public
Body/Government Entity, an Indian Tribe, or a Nonprofit entity
primarily serving rural areas." The FY2026 stakeholder announcement
(https://www.rd.usda.gov/media/file/download/rbcs-rbdg-sa-05182026.pdf)
lists "towns, and communities" among eligible applicants. Projects
must benefit rural areas "outside the urbanized periphery of any city
with a population of 50,000 or more."

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston is a public body in a rural area and
qualifies. (Check the USDA eligibility map for the town's location
relative to the City of Kingston's urbanized periphery — the city has
well under 50,000 residents, so this should not be an issue, but
confirm.)

## Fit

A fit if the town wants a small economic-development planning or
feasibility study (e.g. a hamlet revitalization or agritourism plan),
or a modest facility that supports local businesses. Whether the town
has such an interest needs confirmation. The explicit preference for
smaller requests favors a town this size.

## Effort

Program page steps: contact the NY RD program official before
completing forms; register in SAM.gov (free, takes time); submit
through grants.gov by 4:30 p.m. local time on the deadline. Preparation
"can take a few weeks to several months." A board resolution and a
description of the businesses to be served are standard.
