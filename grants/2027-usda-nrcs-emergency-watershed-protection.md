## Summary

Post-event assistance to relieve "imminent threats to life and
property" caused by floods, fires, windstorms and other natural
disasters that impair a watershed. No disaster declaration is
required. Eligible work: removing debris from stream channels, road
culverts and bridges; reshaping and protecting eroded streambanks;
correcting damaged drainage facilities; establishing vegetative cover
on critically eroding land; repairing levees and structures; repairing
certain conservation practices; and EWP buyouts. Projects must provide
flood or erosion protection, reduce threats to life and property,
restore hydraulic capacity, and be economically and environmentally
defensible. Landowners cannot apply directly; a public sponsor
applies on their behalf.

## Eligibility

Program page (read 2026-11-04): "Eligible sponsors include cities,
counties, towns, conservation districts, and federally recognized
Native American tribes or tribal organizations," and legal subdivisions
of state or tribal government.

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston qualifies as a sponsor. The trigger
is an actual storm/flood event that leaves debris or erosion
threatening roads, culverts, or property.

## Fit

Directly relevant to a highway department after a flood: culvert and
bridge debris, washed-out banks along town roads. Whether the town has
had a recent qualifying event needs confirmation; the value is in
knowing the program exists before the next storm. The Ulster County
Soil & Water Conservation District is also an eligible sponsor and a
likely partner.

## Effort

Program page: "Contact your local service center to start your
application"; NRCS lists state EWP Program Managers. The sponsor must
provide the non-federal share, land rights, permits, and ongoing O&M.
The page does not state a filing deadline after an event; ask the NY
NRCS office how quickly a request must follow the damage.
