## Summary

Grants to rural communities that have experienced — or face imminent —
a significant decline in the quantity or quality of drinking water due
to an emergency: drought, flood, earthquake, tornado/hurricane, disease
outbreak, chemical spill, leak or seepage, or other disasters. Two
grant types: water transmission line grants (up to $150,000) for
waterline extensions, repairing breaks or leaks and related
maintenance; and water source grants (up to $1,000,000) for new wells,
reservoirs, transmission lines, treatment plants, and other sources up
to and including the treatment plant. "A federal disaster declaration
is not required." Matching funds are not required.

## Eligibility

From the program page (read 2026-11-04): applicants are "Rurally
located public bodies," "Nonprofit organizations," and "Federally
recognized tribes." Eligible areas: "Rural areas and towns with
populations of 10,000 or less" with "median household income
less-than the state's median household income for non-metropolitan
areas." Applicants must show "a major decline in quantity or quality
of water occurred within two years of the date of the application" (or
that one is imminent).

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston is a rural public body under 10,000
and qualifies on applicant type. Two further tests need confirmation:
(a) town MHI below NY's nonmetro median, and (b) an actual or imminent
water emergency affecting a water system the town is responsible for.

## Fit

Only relevant if the town operates a community water system (needs
confirmation) and that system suffers a qualifying event. Worth
keeping on file as a known response to a drought, flood, or
contamination event rather than as a routine application.

## Effort

Program page: contact a state program official before completing
forms; apply through RD Apply or the local RD office. No national
architectural/engineering/environmental requirements beyond any
state-specific ones. Must document the water decline and its cause
within the two-year window.
