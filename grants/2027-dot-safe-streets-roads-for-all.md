## Summary

SS4A funds local road-safety work in two tiers: Planning and
Demonstration grants (developing a Safety Action Plan, supplemental
planning, and quick-build demonstration projects) and Implementation
grants for projects identified in an adopted plan. The FY2026 NOFO
(https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-03/SS4A-FY26-NOFO.pdf)
offered up to $993.5M — $305.7M for Planning & Demonstration with
400–700 expected awards in a $100k–$5M range — with a single deadline
of May 26, 2026. DOT announced 297 FY2026 awards on October 30, 2026,
52% benefiting rural communities. The IIJA appropriated SS4A for
2022–2026 only; the FY2026 NOFO says unawarded "Projects of Merit"
may be carried forward "under a future SS4A NOFO, subject to
availability of appropriations," but DOT's page does not say whether
another round will be held.

## Eligibility

FY2026 NOFO Section C.1 (read 2026-11-04): eligible applicants
include "a political subdivision of a State or territory," defined in
Section B as "A unit of government created under the authority of
State law. This includes cities, towns, counties, special districts,
certain transit agencies, and similar units of local government."
Federal share is capped at 80%.

**Verdict:** The Town of Kingston is eligible to apply directly. A
first-time applicant would apply for a Planning grant to develop a
Safety Action Plan (or join a county/regional plan), since
Implementation requires an existing plan.

## Fit

A Safety Action Plan for a town of 900 is a small-dollar request
($100k floor); a town-only plan may be weak against county-level
applications. Whether Ulster County
already has an SS4A-funded Safety Action Plan that covers the town
needs confirmation — if so, the town could apply for supplemental
planning or a demonstration project under it.

## Effort

Per the FY2026 "What's New"
(https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2026-03/SS4A-FY26-Whats-New.pdf):
60-day window, NOFO on grants.gov but applications submitted through
Valid Eval. Needs SAM.gov/UEI, a 20% match commitment, and for
planning grants a scope for the Action Plan. Board resolution
authorizing the application is the normal practice.
